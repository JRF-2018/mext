# Op-ed: Our city center should be car-free

(A fictional op-ed written as a test document for mext. The figures are illustrative, not from real studies.)

Our city should close its downtown core to private cars within five years.

First, safety. Most serious pedestrian injuries in our city happen in the downtown core, and nearly all of them involve private cars. Removing the cars would remove most of the danger.

Second, business. Shop owners fear that customers who drive will stop coming. But surveys in other cities suggest that pedestrians visit shops more often and spend about as much per month as drivers do. A car-free center could therefore help local shops rather than hurt them.

Third, air quality. Traffic is the largest source of nitrogen dioxide downtown, and the levels on our main street regularly exceed the national guideline.

Some will object that people with disabilities depend on cars. This is a real concern, but it is an argument for exceptions, not against the plan: permits for disabled drivers, delivery vehicles and emergency services can be kept.

For these reasons, the city council should adopt a car-free downtown plan.
